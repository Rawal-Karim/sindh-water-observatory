Each day is checked against the Sentinel-2 scene classification (SCL) at every 20 m pixel of your area; a day needs at least 99% of the pixels clear, the same rule as the Earth Engine processor. The few masked pixels are left out of the water total. Earth Engine also applies Cloud Score+, so in rare cases it may still reject a day. The map stays unchanged until results are loaded.

READING THE MAP

Observation ≠ reconstruction.

Detected water: Sentinel-2 MNDWI uses green and shortwave infrared reflectance at 20 m. Cloud and shadow pixels are excluded. Sentinel-1 radar uses a separate, adjustable backscatter threshold.

Most recent available: a rolling observation window supplies the newest valid pixel, so dates can vary across Sindh. A scene date is not province-wide coverage.

Reconstructed surface: unchanged land retains the satellite basemap at its native zoom detail. Synthetic water is clipped to the detected mask. Pixels classified wet before the observation window and dry now receive historical Sentinel fill. These dry patches remain lower resolution. The comparison is against prior Sentinel observations, not the unknown date of the basemap.

Structure protection: mapped bridge decks, barrages, dams, weirs, piers and river-training structures are cut out of the display overlays to reveal the original satellite reference. Line widths are estimated when footprints are unavailable. This does not establish whether a structure is currently flooded or safe. Area totals retain the raw satellite classification. Data: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

This is an experimental reconstruction, not a current photograph or a validated flood-warning service. Radar can confuse smooth dry ground with water, and miss flooded vegetation.

Surface overlays are displayed above the satellite reference without downloading or editing its imagery. Fine detail under former water requires separately licensed high-resolution dry imagery.

Boundary: geoBoundaries gbOpen, PAK ADM1 (public domain). Sentinel: Copernicus / ESA, via Google Earth Engine.
